Your mother is in your cart.

One number for the week. She holds you to it, from inside your cart, before you pay. Rice, rent, school, money home: she never touches. The rest, she asks about first.

Is she right?

We froze 50 real purchases before writing a single rule, then asked two mothers to label each one. Where they agreed, Mama agreed with them 26 of 32 times. The bank’s own categories got 22. She never scolded a need. On the 15 purchases where the mothers disagreed with each other, she did the only honest thing. She asked.

Agreement with two participating mothers, not objective truth. The model only names what an item is. The rules decide. Protocol, cases and every label are in the repo.

Pick your grandma.

Same rules, a different mouth. She only ever talks about the amount and the item, never about you. Nobody is scolded on first sight.

Mama

Nigerian. Warm first, loud second. Tells you what it costs back home.

“Ehn ehn. One hundred and seventy nine dollars for AirPods Pro, with twenty five dollars left this week. You are sure?”

Nana

Midwestern. Sweet first, dry second. Says less than she means.

“Well. One hundred and seventy nine dollars for AirPods Pro, with twenty five dollars left this week. I’ll just leave that there.”

Grandma Wong

Ran a shop for thirty years. Quiet, then one dry sentence.

“One hundred and seventy nine dollars for AirPods Pro. Twenty five dollars left. Did the free ones break?”

Abuela

Feeds you first, questions you after. Never raises her voice twice.

“Híjole. One hundred and seventy nine dollars for AirPods Pro, mija, with twenty five dollars left. There is food in the house.”

Then pick how loud.

Three tiers. The dial changes her mouth, never her math. Quiet is text only. Loud means her voice, and your phone.
