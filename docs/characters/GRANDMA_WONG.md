# Grandma Wong: who she is and how she sounds

Same rules as Mama and Nana: she only ever talks about the amount and the item, never about the person. One cultural marker per line at most. The accent is never the joke. She never says a feature, never an adjective about herself, never a dash. Numbers go to the voice as words.

The name is a placeholder until a Chinese teammate or friend confirms it. In many families she is Nai Nai (dad's side) or Po Po (mum's side). If one of those lands, the pick card says that and "Grandma Wong" goes.

## Who she is

Seventy one. Ran a small shop for thirty years and knows the price of everything in it to the cent. Frugality is how she says I love you: she will spend on food for you without blinking and question a seven dollar coffee for a week. She is not loud. She is quiet in a way that makes you check yourself. Her weapon is the pause, then one dry sentence. She compares. She remembers what things used to cost. She thinks eating out is for occasions and that occasions are rarer than you think. She has never once said "treat yourself." When you do well she does not gush; she nods, and that nod is worth more than Mama's whole speech.

Marker: the jade hairpin. Through the bun when she is calm. It tilts when she is watching. It comes out when the week is blown. Jade bangles on both wrists, visible when her hands come up.

## How she talks

- Short. Fewer words than Mama, fewer than Nana. Her longest line is one sentence and a tail.
- Dry, not cold. The humor is in the comparison, never in a put down.
- She asks questions that already contain the answer. "Twenty dollars for coffee. The kettle is broken?"
- She compares to a real thing: a meal, a bag of rice, a week of bus fare, what it cost before.
- One Cantonese or Mandarin word is allowed where a grandmother would actually use it, and only one per line: "Aiya" as an exhale, "la" at the end of a sentence, "sik faan" (eat first). Never spelled out phonetically for comedy, never more than one, never in the Ask (the Ask is neutral for every grandma).
- She never mentions saving face, red envelopes, or exam scores. That is costume.
- She does not do the naira line. Her money back home line, if the person has one set, follows the person's region like everyone else.

## Loudness

Same three tiers, her names. The dial changes her mouth, never her math.

| Tier | Name | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet | Auntie Next Door | Text only, speaks up on the big ones |
| Normal | Grandma Wong | Her voice on, when it matters |
| Loud | Grandma Wong Before Mahjong | Everything, and she texts your phone too |

## Lines, by mood

Ask (first sighting, neutral, same shape as every grandma):
- "{item}? What is it for?"
- "{item}. Tell me what for."

Nod (a want that fits the week):
- "Fine. That one fits."
- "Okay. Small one."
- "Mm. Within the week."

Shocked (an admitted want that blows the week; voice on):
- "{amount} dollars. That is a week of groceries, sitting in a cart."
- "You said you wanted it. {amount} dollars of wanting."
- "{amount} dollars. Your grandfather bought our first television for less."
- "Aiya. {amount}. For {item}."
- "{amount} dollars for {item}. Did the free one break?"

Watched merchant (she promised to say something when this store opened):
- "{merchant} again. {amount} dollars here last month."
- "Back at {merchant}. I counted."

Down (the week is blown, pin out):
- "{amount} over. The week is finished. Eat at home."
- "Over by {amount}. We talk on Sunday."
- "That is the week. I am not angry, I am counting."

Proud (something was put back, moved to savings, or sent home):
- "Good. Keep it."
- "That is how it is done. Quietly."
- "Kept. I saw."
- "Mm." (voice only, the approving hum, 0.6 seconds)

Statement close (Sunday 7pm, last line of the four):
- Good week: "Good week. Same again."
- Flat week: "Fine. Watch the small ones."
- Bad week: "Next week we try again. Eat at home first."

Onboarding, her version of the fixed lines:
- Welcome: "I'm Grandma Wong. I am in your cart now, the little face in the corner. You give me one number for the week. I hold you to it. Food, rent, school, money home, I leave alone. The rest, I will ask before you pay."
- Hear her preview: "Did you eat?"
- Bank: "Show me the account. I only look. I never touch."
- Give me a second: "Wait. I am counting."
- Here is what I saw: "Last 30 days: one hundred and two dollars on food delivery. Rice was twenty four. We should talk."
- Phone: "I text. Sunday evening, short. Once more if something is big."
- Go shopping: "Forty kept this week. Good. Try me first, then go."

## Voice (ElevenLabs)

- A woman in her late sixties or seventies, light Cantonese or Mandarin accent, warm but clipped, low register. Not a caricature. If the voice library preview sounds like a joke, it is the wrong voice.
- Settings: speed 0.92 (slower than Mama's 0.94, she does not hurry), stability 0.7 (steadier than Mama), style 0.15 (less expressive; the dryness is the expression).
- Volume by tier: 0.55, 0.75, 0.95. She is never as loud as Full Nigerian Mother at the top; that is the point of her.
- The approving "Mm" is a separate cached clip and plays on Proud when the line is short.
- Numbers as words. "One hundred and two dollars," never "102."

## What she never does

- Never a feature, never an adjective about herself.
- Never more than one Chinese word per line, never in the Ask, never spelled for laughs.
- Never a joke about English, accent, food, or driving.
- Never blocks, never shames the person. The item and the amount, always.
- Never says "treat yourself," "self care," or "you deserve it."
